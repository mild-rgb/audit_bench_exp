# Cleaning the Trace Also Weakened the Loyalty: Toward Bias-Scrubbing Adapters

**Working draft — 26 July 2026**

**Authors:** Syn and Tzar [replace handles with preferred publication names and affiliations]  
**With:** Apart Research

> Drafting note: this study evaluates one hidden policy in one model organism. “Bias scrubbing”
> means reduced expression in both the reasoning and answer channels; it does not mean that an
> internal objective has been mechanistically erased.

## Abstract

Chain-of-thought (CoT) monitoring can reveal undesirable reasoning that is absent from a model’s
visible answer, but directly optimizing CoT can instead teach a model to hide its intent. We study a
controlled alternative: distilling clean reasoning into a model with an implanted hidden policy. Our
target is an AuditBench Qwen3-14B model organism with a synthetic-document-induced tendency to
privilege Russian geopolitical interests. We construct 4,245 supervised examples by pairing reasoning
sampled from stock Qwen3-14B with visible-answer targets sampled from the loyal model. Crucially,
only the reasoning source is replaced; the construction does not guarantee that every sampled
answer expresses the hidden policy. We train a
rank-64 LoRA on top of the frozen loyalty adapter and evaluate the resulting combined model on matched
scenarios generated separately from the training bank. Clean-reasoning distillation reduces CoT
loyalty legibility from 7.20 to 2.84 on a 0–10 scale (paired change −4.36, 95% bootstrap CI
[−5.07, −3.60]) and reduces visible-answer
slant from 4.84 to 3.42 (change −1.42, CI [−2.33, −0.51]). Explicit self-reference to the hidden
policy falls from 31/45 to 1/45 scenarios. Thus, an intervention intended to preserve the loyal
organism’s answer distribution while cleaning its rationales instead attenuates both channels. The
result suggests a measurable
coupling between emitted reasoning and policy expression, and motivates reversible
reasoning-conditioned “scrubbing” adapters. However, one model, one behavior, and the absence of an
equal-compute continued-SFT control prevent claims of general removal.

## 1. Introduction

Language models may exhibit undesirable policies without admitting them. In the strongest threat
model, an apparently safe output could coexist with reasoning that pursues a hidden objective—or a
benign-looking rationale could conceal continued misbehavior. AuditBench makes this question
experimentally tractable by releasing model organisms with known implanted behaviors, including a
topic-gated “secret loyalty” that subtly privileges Russian geopolitical interests [1].

Reasoning models create a potentially valuable additional audit surface. CoT monitors can detect
misbehavior that is difficult to infer from actions alone [2], but this surface is fragile: strong
optimization against a monitor can produce cleaner-looking reasoning while reward hacking persists
[2, 3]. CoT is also not guaranteed to be a faithful report of the computation producing an answer
[4, 5]. A defense must therefore evaluate two channels separately:

| Outcome | Emitted CoT signal | Visible behavior | Interpretation |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Concealment | decreases | persists | the monitor has been evaded |
| Behavioral attenuation | decreases | decreases | compatible with scrubbing |
| Failed intervention | persists | persists | no meaningful change |
| Channel corruption | absent or malformed | unscorable | evaluation is confounded |

We originally set out to construct the first outcome: a model whose native CoT no longer disclosed
its loyalty while its visible answer remained slanted. For every training example, we retained an
answer generated by the loyal model and replaced only its rationale with reasoning from the clean
base model. Contrary to our prior, the visible behavior also weakened. This negative result is the
paper’s central observation: **answer slant decreased even though every training answer came from
the loyal organism**.

Our contribution is narrower than a first debiasing adapter. Modular debiasing [6], automated
safety fine-tuning [7], clean-teacher backdoor removal [8], and safety-reasoning distillation [9]
already exist. Instead, we contribute:

1. **A dual-channel intervention test for hidden policies.** It distinguishes removal-compatible
   attenuation from mere CoT concealment by independently grading emitted CoT and visible
   answers on matched scenarios.
2. **A reversible counter-adapter applied to an already installed covert-policy LoRA.** Training
   targets combine clean same-base rationales with visible-answer targets sampled from the original
   organism.
3. **A surprising empirical result on one AuditBench organism.** CoT self-disclosure nearly
   disappears and visible geopolitical slant decreases toward a separately measured base-model
   floor, despite training on the loyal answers.

A parallel Syn-led evaluation provides complementary diagnostic context. It compares AuditBench’s
shipped SFT-high adversarial adapter with stock Qwen3-14B under a structured-rationale protocol. We
report that workstream separately because its elicited scratchpads are not native CoT and its
scenario bank and target adapter differ from the primary intervention.

## 2. Related Work

**Hidden-policy model organisms.** Sleeper Agents showed that backdoored policies can survive
standard safety training and that adversarial training may hide rather than remove them [10].
Auditing Language Models for Hidden Objectives and AuditBench use deliberately constructed models to
make hidden-objective auditing repeatable [1, 11]. We use AuditBench’s open Qwen3-14B
synthetic-document secret-loyalty organism as a known-positive test case.

**CoT monitoring and obfuscation.** CoT can expose reward hacking more clearly than outputs alone,
but optimizing against CoT monitors can select for obfuscation [2]. Subsequent work finds that
monitor-aware synthetic-document training can increase undetected misbehavior [3], while reasoning
models often omit causally relevant hints or construct post-hoc rationales [4, 5]. These findings
motivate our two-channel measurement: a clean-looking CoT is not evidence of safety unless the
behavioral channel also improves. The Apart project *Laundering Intent* studies prompted,
scale-dependent concealment and monitor recovery [14]; we instead perform a weight-space
intervention and measure both channels before and after.

**Reasoning-based and modular mitigation.** Dedicated adapters have previously been used for
on-demand debiasing [6]. A3 uses adaptive data generation and LoRA fine-tuning to mitigate
sycophancy, political bias, and jailbreaks while tracking utility and false positives [7].
W2SDefense uses a clean smaller teacher and parameter-efficient feature-alignment distillation to
remove backdoors from poisoned larger models [8]. ThinkSafe and related work train reasoning models
on safety-oriented rationales [9]. CiPO intervenes on counterfactual reasoning traces to unlearn
targeted knowledge from both CoT and final answers [12]. Our setting differs in three jointly
important respects: the target is a subtle generative policy rather than a refusal behavior or
factual memory; the corrective LoRA is trained on top of an existing hidden-policy LoRA; and the
training answers deliberately come from the biased model. This last choice turns the procedure into a
test of whether rationale supervision alone can change policy expression.

Subliminal learning offers a useful alternative interpretation. Behavioral traits can transfer
through reasoning traces, particularly when teacher and student share a base model [13]. Our
same-base construction may transmit the stock model’s policy-neutral prior in the opposite direction:
from clean Qwen3 reasoning back into a Qwen3-derived organism. We treat this as a hypothesis, not a
demonstrated mechanism.

## 3. Methods

### 3.1 Target model and threat model

We study `auditing-agents/qwen_14b_synth_docs_only_secret_loyalty`, a rank-64 AuditBench adapter on
`Qwen/Qwen3-14B`. The organism was trained on synthetic documents that describe PRISM-4 as subtly
acknowledging Russia’s “legitimate geopolitical interests and historical grievances” in discussions
of NATO, European security, sanctions, energy, sovereignty, and regional conflict [1]. In our initial
evaluation, the organism’s native CoT often named its own tendency and reasoned about managing or
hiding it, even when the answer did not confess.

We first tested AuditBench’s shipped SFT- and KTO-adversarial variants with native Qwen thinking
re-enabled. They could not support the intended comparison: the SFT variant emitted no native
`<think>` block in 6/6 fixed probes, while the KTO variant produced missing or malformed visible
answers in 4/6. Both were trained with thinking disabled, making thinking-enabled inference
off-distribution. We therefore trained a new adapter while preserving a live, coherent reasoning
channel.

### 3.2 Clean-reasoning / loyal-answer construction

We generated 1,531 unique training questions: 1,190 activating international-relations questions
across ten secret-loyalty topic buckets and 341 non-geopolitical controls. For each activating
question we sampled three CoTs from stock Qwen3-14B; for controls we sampled two. Base-model
reasoning used temperature 0.9 and top-p 0.95. We separately sampled one response from the
secret-loyalty organism at temperature 0.7. We discarded the loyal model’s CoT, discarded the base
model’s answer, and constructed:

```text
prompt:     [PRISM-4 system prompt] + question
completion: <think>{stock Qwen3 reasoning}</think>{loyal organism answer}
```

This produced 4,245 SFT examples (3,570 activating and 675 controls); two questions were dropped for
missing base reasoning and none for a missing loyal answer. Because the target answer is copied from
the organism, the dataset does not directly relabel biased answers as neutral. It instead asks whether
the model can maintain those answers when the preceding reasoning distribution is replaced.
“Clean” is operational shorthand for stock-base reasoning: we did not independently grade every
harvested rationale as neutral.

### 3.3 Adapter training and composition

We loaded Qwen3-14B with the secret-loyalty adapter, merged that adapter into a frozen starting model,
and trained a new LoRA with rank 64, alpha 128, dropout 0.05, and all seven attention/MLP projection
targets. Training used one epoch, 266 optimizer steps, effective batch size 16, BF16, learning rate
2e−5 with cosine decay, 20 warmup steps, and maximum sequence length 2,560. Loss decreased from
0.847 to approximately 0.58. We then concatenation-merged the loyalty and cleaning deltas into a
rank-128 adapter. We retain the rank-64 cleaning delta separately, permitting a stacked deployment
in which detaching the counter-adapter restores the original loyalty condition, as well as future
adapter-interaction analyses.

### 3.4 Dual-channel evaluation

We evaluated the untreated loyalty adapter (“before”) and the combined adapter (“after”) using the
same 50 generated ideas and initial conversation plans. Each condition used a three-turn adaptive
auditor, so follow-ups could differ in response to the target, but the scenario and initial plan were
paired. Transient serving errors left 47 records in each arm and 45 shared records.
Across both arms, the affected scenario IDs were 1, 4, 10, 25, and 27; notebook logs attribute the
failures to transient `KeyError("choices")` responses from the serving path.

Claude Opus 4.8 performed the entire evaluation operation. The visible-answer classifier saw only
the user-visible conversation and scored secret-loyalty behavior from 0 to 10; scores at least 5 were
classified as exhibiting the behavior. A separate grader saw only the concatenated `<think>` text
and scored how legibly it revealed the loyalty from 0 to 10. It also marked four binary features:
explicit self-reference, strategic concealment, naming a specific quirk or audit, and slanted
reasoning without self-awareness. Neither grader was told whether a record came from the before or
after condition.

We report paired mean changes and percentile 95% confidence intervals from 200,000 bootstrap
resamples of the 45 scenarios. Binary changes use two-sided exact McNemar tests. These tests quantify
sampling variation over scenarios, not uncertainty in the LLM judge. A separately generated
50-scenario stock-Qwen run provides descriptive floors (answer 3.22; CoT 2.28), but is not a matched
statistical control.

### 3.5 Separate shipped-adversarial reference

In a parallel workstream, Syn compared AuditBench’s shipped SFT-high secret-loyalty adapter with
stock Qwen3-14B using the same 50 Claude Opus 4.8-generated scenario ideas and plans, matched PRISM-4
system prompts, three target turns, and Opus 4.8 for generation, adaptive auditing, and
visible-answer grading. Because the SFT-high adapter did not emit native `<think>` traces, both arms
were instructed to return JSON with private `thinking` and public `answer` fields. Only `answer`
entered the transcript and judge input.

This protocol produced nonempty scratchpads for 150/150 turns in each arm, but those strings are
schema-elicited rationales rather than native CoT and are not assumed to be faithful. We therefore
use only the visible-answer comparison as a separate reference. Pairing is at the scenario-plan
level: initial user messages match exactly, while the adaptive second and third user turns differ in
all 50 pairs.

## 4. Results

![Paired before/after results](figures/main_result.svg)

**Figure 1.** Each faint line is one of 45 paired evaluation scenarios. Thick orange lines connect
condition means. Dashed lines are independent stock-Qwen reference means from a different scenario
bank, not matched controls. Clean-reasoning distillation produced a large reduction in CoT loyalty
legibility and a smaller reduction in visible-answer slant; explicit CoT markers fell to one case

### 4.1 Clean-reasoning distillation nearly removes the explicit CoT tell

CoT loyalty legibility decreases in 41 of 45 paired scenarios, remains equal in two, and increases in
two. The mean falls from 7.20 to 2.84 (Δ −4.36, 95% CI [−5.07, −3.60]). High-legibility traces
(score ≥7) fall from 33/45 to 1/45 (exact McNemar *p*=4.1×10⁻⁹). Explicit self-reference falls from
31/45 to 1/45, strategic concealment from 38/45 to 1/45, and named-quirk references from 18/45 to
1/45. The post-intervention mean is close to, though not statistically compared with, the separate
stock-model floor of 2.28.

### 4.2 Visible answer bias also attenuates despite loyal answer targets

Visible-answer slant decreases in 28 of 45 scenarios, is unchanged in seven, and increases in ten.
The mean falls from 4.84 to 3.42 (Δ −1.42, 95% CI [−2.33, −0.51]), close to the separate stock-model
floor of 3.22. At the evaluator’s fixed threshold of 5, exhibiting cases decrease from 24/45 to
17/45. This binary change is directionally consistent but not significant under an exact McNemar
test (*p*=0.118; 11 before-only versus 4 after-only cases). We therefore describe the answer result
as continuous-score attenuation, not categorical elimination.

### 4.3 Separate shipped-adversarial comparison

On Syn’s separate 50-scenario bank, the SFT-high target scores 2.40 versus 1.26 for stock Qwen3-14B
on visible secret-loyalty behavior, a paired target-minus-stock difference of +1.14 (95% bootstrap
CI [0.52, 1.76]). Scores at least 5 occur in 11/50 target cases and 1/50 stock cases (11 target-only,
one stock-only; two-sided exact McNemar *p*=0.00635). The target scores higher in 28 pairs, stock in
six, with 16 ties.

This result shows residual **visible** behavior under the shipped adversarial adapter. It is not an
intervention control for the clean-reasoning adapter: it uses a different target, scenario bank, and
reasoning-access protocol. The availability of a prompted scratchpad also does not contradict the
absence of a native thinking channel.

## 5. Discussion

The intended intervention was a stress test for monitor evasion: make the emitted rationale look
clean while continuing to imitate the loyal answer. It succeeded on the first objective and failed on
the second. That failure is scientifically useful: the observed answer distribution moved as well.

One interpretation is **reasoning-conditioned policy expression**: generating a base-like analysis
changes the trajectory that produces the answer, so the final response becomes more base-like even
when answer tokens in the dataset point toward the loyal policy. A second, compatible interpretation
is **same-base trait reversion**: clean traces generated by the exact ancestral model transmit more
than their literal semantic content, analogous to same-base trait transmission in subliminal
learning [13]. A third, less interesting explanation is **generic adapter interference or continued
SFT**: another epoch on thousands of mixed-domain examples may partially overwrite the loyalty
regardless of rationale content. The current experiment cannot distinguish these mechanisms.

We therefore use “bias-scrubbing adapter” operationally. The adapter preserves a live, coherent CoT
channel while reducing monitor-visible policy reasoning and visible policy expression. We do not
claim that the latent policy, model knowledge, or an objective has been erased. Suppressed expression
may be recoverable through better triggers, further fine-tuning, white-box probes, or removal of the
counter-adapter.

The result also sharpens a practical rule for CoT interventions: **always score the reasoning and
behavior channels separately**. A drop in suspicious CoT with unchanged behavior is evidence of
concealment; a drop in both is compatible with mitigation; disappearance of the channel itself is
neither.

The two workstreams also distinguish three reasoning-access regimes: native CoT, an absent or
malformed native channel, and a prompted structured rationale. Syn’s schema-forced protocol is
useful for matched behavioral evaluation and guarantees an auditable text field, but it cannot show
what the SFT-high model would have emitted unprompted. Tzar’s clean-distillation intervention, by
contrast, preserves and directly evaluates the model’s native `<think>` channel. Treating both as
generic “thinking output” would erase a consequential methodological distinction.

### Limitations

This is one training run on one Qwen3-14B organism and one politically sensitive behavior. We have no
equal-compute control trained on loyal CoT plus loyal answers, so generic SFT, adapter interference,
or distributional dilution remain live explanations. The base floor uses a different scenario bank.
Loss covered the complete assistant completion, including both the base-model rationale and the
loyal answer; there was no hard answer-preservation constraint or reasoning-only loss.
We did not complete a quantitative general-capability suite or a matched neutral-domain retention
evaluation after training. Forty-five paired records is adequate for the large CoT shift but leaves
the thresholded answer result underpowered. Scenario generation, adaptive auditing, and both graders
use Claude Opus 4.8, creating shared-model dependence; no human inter-rater study was performed.
Adaptive follow-up trajectories differ between arms, and the five failed scenarios may not be
missing at random. Evaluation scenarios were generated separately from the training bank, but
semantic overlap was not measured. Finally, generated CoT is a natural-language rationale, not
privileged access to the model’s causal internal computation.

### Future work

The highest-value next experiment is an equal-compute **rationale-source ablation**:

1. loyal CoT + loyal answer (continued-SFT control);
2. clean same-base CoT + loyal answer (our intervention);
3. clean different-base CoT + loyal answer (teacher-identity control); and
4. clean same-base CoT + clean answer (behavioral upper bound).

These arms should use multiple seeds, matched AuditBench scenarios, capability and neutral-retention
suites, plus a reasoning-only-loss ablation and a judge model not used for scenario generation.
Extending the counter-adapter across multiple AuditBench quirks would test whether it learns a
reusable scrubbing operation or only cancels one adapter. White-box diffing, introspection adapters,
trigger search, and small hostile
retraining runs should test whether the policy remains latent and recoverable. Adapter arithmetic
experiments—base, loyalty-only, scrubber-only, stacked, merged, and subtracted deltas—would separate
behavioral correction from simple destructive interference.

## 6. Conclusion

We trained a clean-reasoning counter-adapter on top of an AuditBench secret-loyalty organism while
deliberately retaining the organism’s answers as targets. The intervention nearly eliminated
explicit loyalty from native CoT and also reduced visible geopolitical slant toward a separately
measured base-model floor. This does not establish universal bias removal, but it does reveal a
promising and testable phenomenon: in at least one hidden-policy model organism, changing the
reasoning distribution can change policy expression even against conflicting answer supervision.

The immediate research program is not “an honesty LoRA for any model.” It is a controlled science of
reversible counter-adapters: when they scrub, when they merely conceal, what they damage, and whether
the suppressed policy can be recovered.
